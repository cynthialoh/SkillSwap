

# **SkillSwap — Product Requirements Document (PRD)**

## **1\. Product Overview**

**Product Name:** SkillSwap

**Tagline:**

> **Your Skills Are Currency.**

**Product Concept:**  
SkillSwap is a global skill-exchange marketplace where people can **teach what they know and learn what they want**.

Users can exchange skills directly with one another or earn **Skill Credits** by helping others and use those credits to learn from someone else.

For example:

> Sarah teaches photography and wants to learn Excel.  
> John teaches Excel and wants to learn photography.

SkillSwap recognizes the connection and recommends them to each other.

Users can also choose paid sessions when they prefer to learn from someone without exchanging skills.

---

# **2\. Problem**

Millions of people possess valuable skills but don't have an easy way to exchange them.

At the same time, people want to learn new skills but:

* Courses can be expensive.  
* Finding the right person can be difficult.  
* Social media isn't designed for structured skill exchanges.  
* Freelance marketplaces focus primarily on buying and selling services.  
* Traditional learning platforms don't facilitate person-to-person skill exchange.

### **The opportunity**

Create a platform where:

> **Knowledge, experience and time become exchangeable value.**

---

# **3\. Product Vision**

Build a global community where anyone can say:

> **"This is what I know. This is what I want to learn."**

And SkillSwap helps them find the right person.

The long-term vision is to become a global marketplace and community for **peer-to-peer learning, skill exchange and knowledge sharing.**

---

# **4\. User Groups**

SkillSwap has exactly **2 user groups**:

### **Group 1: Members (Learners / Teachers)**

A single unified group. Every Member can both teach and learn.

A Member has two sides to their profile:

* **I CAN TEACH** — skills they offer
* **I WANT TO LEARN** — skills they want to acquire

Members can:

* Create and edit their Skill Profile
* Browse/search people, skills, categories, opportunities
* Receive smart matches
* Send/accept SkillSwap requests
* Chat, agree, schedule, exchange
* Earn/spend Skill Credits
* Leave/receive ratings & reviews
* Report/block other Members

Example Members:

**The Professional**

> "I'm good at Excel and want to learn photography."

**The Hobbyist**

> "I know how to bake cakes and want to learn graphic design."

**The Student**

> "I can teach mathematics and want to learn coding."

**The Beginner**

> "I don't have professional qualifications, but I know how to cook African food really well."

### **Group 2: Admins**

Platform operators. Admins do not exchange skills — they manage the platform.

Admins can:

* Manage users (view, verify, suspend, ban)
* Manage skill categories and listings
* Manage Skill Credits (view balances, adjust, resolve disputes)
* Moderate reports, reviews, community posts
* View platform metrics (users, matches, completed exchanges, retention)
* Configure trust & safety rules and verification requirements

# **10\. SkillSwap Exchange Flow**

The core user journey:

### **1\. Discover**

Find a potential match.

↓

### **2\. Request**

Send a SkillSwap request.

↓

### **3\. Chat**

Discuss what each person wants.

↓

### **4\. Agree**

Confirm what will be exchanged.

↓

Choose a suitable time.

↓

### **6\. Exchange**

Complete the learning session.

↓

> **Discover → Match → Connect → Exchange → Review**

# **11\. Skill Credits**

SkillSwap introduces its own internal exchange currency:

## **Skill Credits**

The basic principle:

> **1 hour of helping someone \= 1 Skill Credit**

Example:

You teach Excel for 2 hours.

**You earn: 2 Skill Credits.**

You then use those credits to learn photography for 2 hours.

### **User dashboard**

**Skill Credits: 14**

**Hours taught:** 18

**Hours learned:** 11

**Successful exchanges:** 23

Skill Credits should initially represent **time and contribution**, rather than being directly convertible to cash.

# **12\. Direct Skill Exchange**

Users can also exchange skills directly.

Example:

> "I'll teach you Excel for one hour if you teach me photography for one hour."

No credits are necessary.

This creates two exchange methods:

### **Direct Swap**

**Skill ↔ Skill**

### **Credit Swap**

**Skill → Credits → Skill**

# **14\. User Profiles & Roles**

### **14a. Member Profile (Learner / Teacher)**

A Member SkillSwap profile should communicate:

### **About Me**

Short introduction.

### **I Can Teach**

List of skills.

### **I Want to Learn**

Desired skills.

### **Experience**

User's experience level.

### **Verification**

Identity and/or skill verification badges.

### **Reviews**

Feedback from previous exchanges.

### **Exchange History**

Number of completed exchanges.

### **Skill Credits**

Current balance.

### **Availability**

When the user is available.

### **Learning Preferences**

Online, in-person or either.

### **14b. Admin Profile**

Admins have an operational account, not a Skill Profile:

* Admin dashboard access
* User management queue (verifications, reports, appeals)
* Content/category management
* Credit ledger and dispute resolution
* Platform analytics
* No Teach/Learn skills, no Skill Credits balance, no public profile by default.

---

# **15\. Trust & Safety**

Trust is critical because Members may meet or interact with strangers.

### **15a. Member tools**

SkillSwap should provide to Members:

### **Identity Verification**

Users can verify their identity.

### **Skill Verification**

Where appropriate, users can provide evidence of qualifications or experience.

### **Ratings & Reviews**

Both participants review each other after an exchange.

### **Exchange History**

Users can see how many exchanges someone has successfully completed.

### **Report**

Users can report inappropriate behavior.

### **Block**

Users can block other users.

### **Trust Indicators**

Profiles can display:

✓ Identity Verified  
✓ Skill Verified  
⭐ Highly Rated  
🔄 Exchanges Completed

### **15b. Admin enforcement**

Admins handle:

* Reviewing identity/skill verification requests
* Reviewing reports, warnings, suspensions, bans
* Removing abusive reviews or listings
* Resolving credit/exchange disputes
* Audit log of all admin actions

# **21\. Competitive Differentiation**

SkillSwap is not simply:

**A course platform**

because users teach each other.

It's not simply:

**A freelance marketplace**

because users can exchange skills without money.

It's not simply:

**A social network**

because interactions have a practical purpose.

It's not simply:

**A tutoring platform**

because users can both teach and learn.

### **The core difference:**

> **SkillSwap turns people's skills and time into something they can exchange.**

# **26\. The Big Idea**

The strongest part of SkillSwap isn't the matching.

It is this:

> ### **Everyone has something valuable to teach.**

> **"How much does it cost?"**

> **"What can you exchange for it?"**

That gives SkillSwap a distinctive identity and a potentially powerful community-driven business model.
